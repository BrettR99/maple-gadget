1440p is the sweet spot for PC gaming. It's a big step up from 1080p in sharpness, and most mid-range graphics cards can still push high frame rates at this resolution. The catch in Canada is that budget 1440p stock rotates fast: models go in and out of listings, and the price you see this week might be gone next week.

Every monitor below typically sells for under CA$400, and every retailer link was verified in September 2026. Where a listing was thin or a price only shows at checkout, we say so instead of pretending otherwise.

## How this roundup was built

This is a **research roundup, not a hands-on review**. Maple Gadget didn't receive or test these monitors in person. Our launch budget is $0, and we won't pretend otherwise. Instead, the rankings below are built from:

- **Measurement-based reviews** from outlets with real testing rigs: RTINGS, Tom's Hardware, PCWorld, PC Gamer, PCGamesN, TechSpot, and Tech Advisor
- **Manufacturer spec sheets**, used for specifications only and never as proof that a product performs well
- **Canadian retail listings** at Best Buy Canada, Amazon.ca, Newegg.ca, and Dell Canada for pricing and availability

What we did *not* use: Amazon customer review text, user star ratings, content-farm "top 10" listicles, AI-generated review spam, or sponsored reviews without clear disclosure. Reddit threads were consulted only as background on common complaints, never as the basis for a ranking.

The criteria, in order: **measured gaming performance** (response time, input lag, motion clarity), **image quality** (SDR first, since that's what you'll actually use), **ergonomics and features**, then **Canadian price and availability**. HDR capability was not treated as a selling point at this price, for reasons explained below.

Scores are Maple Gadget's own judgment based on the evidence above. Nobody paid for placement, and nobody can. We have no affiliate relationships, and rankings are never for sale.

This is the easiest monitor on this list to actually buy in Canada right now, and it's frequently the cheapest good 1440p panel available. At CA$189.99 at Best Buy Canada at the time of checking, this is almost half the price of the Dell while still being a competent 1440p 165Hz gaming panel. If you play in a dark room, the VA contrast is a real, visible advantage.

## What to look for (and what to ignore)

**IPS vs VA is the real tradeoff at this price.** IPS panels (Dell, Gigabyte, LG, ASUS) give faster pixel response and wider viewing angles, but contrast around 1000:1 means blacks look grey in a dark room. VA (Samsung G5) gives roughly triple the contrast for genuinely deeper blacks, but slower dark-scene transitions can smear. Pick based on your room lighting and the games you play, not brand loyalty.

**HDR badges are marketing at this price.** DisplayHDR 400 means the monitor accepts an HDR signal, not that it displays real HDR. With no local dimming and 300–400 nits of brightness, HDR usually looks worse than SDR on these panels. Judge them on SDR picture quality and leave HDR off.

**Use DisplayPort for full refresh rate.** Most of these cap HDMI at 144Hz; the advertised 165Hz+ modes need DisplayPort. If your new monitor seems stuck at 144Hz, the cable is the first thing to check.

**Check the stand before you assume.** Budget 1440p monitors often ship with tilt-only stands and no height adjustment. A basic VESA monitor arm costs around CA$40–60 and fixes it permanently, but factor it into the real price.

**Ignore "1ms" on the box.** Manufacturer response-time claims are best-case figures measured under ideal conditions. Real response times depend on overdrive settings and vary widely, which is why the RTINGS and Tom's Hardware measured results cited above matter more than the spec sheet.

It's the best-measured all-rounder under $400: fast, accurate, fully adjustable, and compatible with every Adaptive Sync standard. Just buy it direct from Dell Canada, since third-party Canadian listings are unreliable.

And if you're a competitive shooter player, wait for a sale on the **LG 27GP850-B** and grab the fastest motion handling in the roundup.
