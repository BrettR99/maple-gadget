Apple's September 9 event had two stories. The loud one was the new hardware: the iPhone 18 Pro, the iPhone 18 Pro Max, and Apple's first foldable, the iPhone Duo. The quiet one happened on apple.com/ca the same day, with no press release and no mention on stage: **Apple raised the price of every older iPhone it still sells.**

This breaks the normal pattern. When new iPhones launch, last year's models are supposed to get cheaper. This year they got more expensive, some by a lot. If your buying strategy was "wait for the new ones, then grab last year's model on discount," that playbook is broken at Apple retail.

**Prices checked September 29, 2026.** The figures below are Apple Store Canada outright prices, as reported by [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/09/09/apple-raises-prices-older-iphone-models-18-pro-duo-reveal/) and [iPhoneInCanada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2026/09/09/apple-raises-iphone-17-iphone-air-and-17e-prices-in-canada-by-up-to-450/) on September 9. This is a news explainer, not a hands-on review: the prices are documented fact, and the buying advice is Maple Gadget's own judgment. Nobody paid for placement, and nobody can.

## Every Canadian price change

**iPhone 17: $1,129 → $1,299 (up $170).** The big one. The phone most people actually buy got $170 more expensive overnight. The 512GB model went from $1,429 to $1,599. Here is the detail that stings: the iPhone 17 launched with a $150 storage-for-storage saving over the iPhone 16. That saving is now gone. Both iPhone 17 configurations cost $20 *more* than the equivalent iPhone 16 models did at their original launch prices.

**iPhone 16: $999 → $1,149 (up $150).** A two-generations-old phone, now $150 more expensive. (It launched at $1,129 and Apple had cut it to $999 earlier; that cut has been more than reversed.)

**iPhone 17e: $899 → $999 (up $100).** Apple's cheapest iPhone now starts a dollar below four figures before tax. The 512GB model went from $1,199 to $1,299.

**iPhone Air: $1,449 → $1,599 (up $150).** And the storage upgrades got hit hardest: the 512GB model went from $1,749 to $1,899, and the 1TB model jumped from $2,049 to **$2,499, a $450 increase**, nearly 22 percent. The gap between 512GB and 1TB doubled from $300 to $600.

**iPhone 18 Pro: $1,749. iPhone 18 Pro Max: $1,899.** The new Pros cost $150 more than the 17 Pros did at launch. For the full breakdown of whether they're worth it, see our [iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max buying guide](https://maplegadget.ca/reviews/iphone-18-pro-pro-max-canada).

**Discontinued:** the iPhone 17 Pro and 17 Pro Max are gone from Apple's lineup entirely, and the iPhone 16 Pro series is gone too. There is no Pro phone from a previous generation left to buy at Apple.

## Why did Apple do this?

Apple said nothing on stage and issued no press release, so there is no official explanation beyond the prices themselves. The widely cited explanation across MobileSyrup, iPhoneInCanada, Cult of Mac, and WhistleOut is **memory chip costs**: the AI boom has created a shortage in DRAM and NAND production, driving up component prices. Apple already raised prices on Macs and iPads back in June; the iPhone was notably left out then, and that has now changed.

Note the exchange rate doesn't explain it. The Canadian dollar was actually slightly *stronger* against the US dollar at this year's event than at last year's, and Apple's implied USD-to-CAD conversion barely moved. This is a cost increase, not a currency adjustment.

## What this means for you

**Stop buying older iPhones at Apple retail.** The old advice, "just buy last year's model," assumed last year's model got cheaper. It didn't. Paying Apple's new $1,299 for an iPhone 17 makes no sense when the 18 Pro is $450 more and a genuine generational camera upgrade.

**The deals moved to carriers and retailers.** The value now lives wherever old stock is being cleared: carrier clearance pages at Rogers, Bell, Telus, and Freedom Mobile, plus Best Buy Canada's clearance and open-box units. Apple raised its prices; everyone clearing inventory did not.

**If you're on an iPhone 15 Pro or older, the 18 Pro is still a good buy.** The variable-aperture camera is the best ever measured on an iPhone (DxOMark 172), and battery life is record-setting. Our [buying guide](https://maplegadget.ca/reviews/iphone-18-pro-pro-max-canada) has the full evidence.

**If you're on a 16 Pro or 17 Pro, keep your phone.** Every major reviewer, The Verge, CNET, Engadget, agrees the upgrades are minor and this generation is a skip. The kindest thing anyone can tell you is: keep your money.

**If you normally buy the regular iPhone, you're waiting.** There is no iPhone 18 until spring 2027 at the earliest, per multiple reports Apple hasn't confirmed. Until then it's a discounted 17 or sitting tight.

**The cheapest upgrade Apple sells is a battery replacement.** If your current phone still works, one more year plus a fresh battery beats paying 2026 prices for 2025 hardware.

## The bottom line

Apple bet that nobody would notice prices going up while everyone was looking at the new phones. People noticed. The iPhone 17 now costs more than the iPhone 16 did at launch, the cheapest iPhone is $999, and a 1TB Air costs $2,499. Buy the new Pros if you're genuinely due for an upgrade, hunt carrier clearance if you want last year's model, and otherwise hold onto what you have. This is not a buyer's market.
