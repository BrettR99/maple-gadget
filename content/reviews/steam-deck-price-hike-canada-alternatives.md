The Steam Deck was the default answer for handheld PC gaming in Canada: cheap, simple, and good enough. That ended on May 27, 2026, when Valve quietly raised Canadian prices by more than 60 percent. The 512GB OLED went from **$689 to $1,129 CAD**, and the 1TB from **$819 to $1,349 CAD**, with the hardware completely unchanged from the 2023 launch. Valve blamed surging memory and storage costs, driven by AI data centres buying up global supply. Sony and Nintendo have since raised PS5 and Switch 2 prices for the same reason.

**Prices checked September 30, 2026.** Canadian prices below come from manufacturer announcements, Best Buy Canada, Amazon.ca, and Canadian outlets (iPhoneInCanada, MobileSyrup, Console Creatures). Handheld prices move around, so treat these as the baseline and check for sales.

## How this roundup was built

This is a **research roundup, not a hands-on review**. Maple Gadget didn't receive or test these handhelds. The rankings are built from:

- **Reviews and reporting** from credible outlets: iPhoneInCanada (Canadian Steam Deck pricing), MobileSyrup, Console Creatures (long-term ROG Ally X testing), TechRepublic, Tech Times, and Tom's Guide-style testing coverage
- **Manufacturer spec pages and press releases** (Valve, ASUS, Lenovo), used for specifications and Canadian pricing only
- **Canadian retail listings** at Best Buy Canada, Amazon.ca, and the ASUS Store

What we did *not* use: sponsored reviews without disclosure, content-farm listicles, or AI-generated review spam.

The criteria, in order: **gaming performance**, **display quality**, **battery life**, **software simplicity**, then **Canadian price**. Scores are Maple Gadget's own judgment. Nobody paid for placement, and nobody can. We have no affiliate relationships, and rankings are never for sale.

## The picks

### 1. ROG Xbox Ally, best value now

**Score: 8.5/10. $799 CAD.**

This is the new budget king, and it wears the crown the Steam Deck left behind. The base ROG Xbox Ally launched at $799 CAD in October 2025, and Best Buy Canada has already discounted it to around **$499** during summer sales, which is $630 less than a Deck OLED. You get Windows 11 with the Xbox full-screen experience, which means native Game Pass, Steam, Epic, and every other launcher with no compatibility layer. The AMD Ryzen Z2 A chip is the entry-level option in the lineup, so temper expectations on the heaviest AAA games, but for the price of a discounted unit it is the easiest recommendation in handheld PC gaming right now.

**Pros:** Cheapest way into handheld PC gaming; native Game Pass and every PC storefront; Xbox full-screen UI is genuinely good; already discounted well below MSRP.

**Cons:** Z2 A is the weakest chip in this roundup; 7-inch IPS screen, no OLED; Windows handhelds need more fiddling than SteamOS.

**Best for:** Anyone who wanted a Steam Deck at the old price. Buy it on sale and don't look back.

**Check price:** [ASUS Canada](https://rog.asus.com/ca-en/gaming-handhelds/rog-ally/rog-xbox-ally-2025/)

### 2. ROG Xbox Ally X, best performance

**Score: 8.5/10. $1,299 CAD.**

The fastest handheld you can actually buy in Canada today. The Ryzen AI Z2 Extreme delivers roughly 30 percent more performance than the previous generation according to ASUS's testing, backed by 24GB of RAM and an 80Wh battery that is nearly double the Steam Deck's. Console Creatures' reviewer used one as a daily driver for a year and called it their favourite handheld over the Steam Deck OLED. At $1,299 it costs $170 more than the hiked Deck OLED 512GB while being faster in every meaningful way, which tells you everything about how much Valve's pricing has shifted the market.

**Pros:** Best performance in a 7-inch handheld; huge 80Wh battery; 24GB RAM; great controls and build.

**Cons:** $1,299 is real money; IPS screen when rivals are going OLED; heavy at 678g.

**Best for:** Performance-first buyers who want the fastest handheld without stepping up to the $1,899 X20.

**Check price:** [ASUS Canada](https://rog.asus.com/ca-en/gaming-handhelds/rog-ally/rog-xbox-ally-x-2025/)

### 3. Lenovo Legion Go S, best SteamOS alternative

**Score: 8.0/10. $1,099 CAD.**

If what you loved about the Deck was SteamOS simplicity rather than the price, this is your pick. The Legion Go S was the first authorized third-party SteamOS handheld, and the 8-inch 120Hz screen is bigger and smoother than the Deck's. The Windows version with a Ryzen Z2 Go, 32GB of RAM, and 1TB SSD sells for $1,099.99 CAD on Amazon.ca. The SteamOS version launched at $499 USD, though Canadian availability has been spotty, so confirm which version you're buying before you pay.

**Pros:** SteamOS option with Deck-like simplicity; bigger 8-inch 120Hz display; 32GB RAM on the Windows model.

**Cons:** Canadian SteamOS availability is inconsistent; Windows version needs the usual handheld Windows fiddling; 55.5Wh battery is the smallest here.

**Best for:** Steam loyalists who want the Deck software experience on newer, bigger hardware.

**Check price:** [Amazon.ca](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B0FHHY5TN5)

## Who should still buy a Steam Deck

**Bargain hunters: the $499 LCD model.** Valve left the 256GB LCD Deck at $499 CAD, and refurbished LCD units on Valve's store still go at old prices when in stock. If you mainly play indies and older games, that's still fine hardware for the money.

**SteamOS diehards with patience:** the Deck OLED remains the most polished SteamOS device, with the best standby battery life and the simplest experience. But at $1,129, only buy it if you specifically want Valve's software and are willing to pay a premium for it. Everyone else should buy from the picks above.

## Who should skip

**Everyone eyeing the ROG Xbox Ally X20 at $1,899:** it looks spectacular (7.4-inch OLED at 120Hz, Z2 Extreme, ships October 15), but that's nearly four times the sale price of the base Ally for diminishing returns. Enthusiast luxury, not a recommendation.

**MSI Claw buyers in Canada:** the Claw 8 AI+ is fast on paper with Intel's Lunar Lake chip, but pricing is erratic, stock is thin, and Intel's handheld driver maturity still trails AMD. Skip until that changes.

**Nintendo-only gamers:** the Switch 2 ($679.99 CAD after its own September price bump) is a great console, but it's not a PC handheld. Different purchase, different library.

## The bottom line

Valve's 60 percent price hike broke the Steam Deck's value proposition in Canada, and the market moved on. The **ROG Xbox Ally** ($799, often $499 on sale) is the new budget king, the **ROG Xbox Ally X** ($1,299) is the performance pick, and the **Lenovo Legion Go S** ($1,099) is the SteamOS alternative. The only Deck worth buying now is the cheapest one: the $499 LCD, preferably refurbished.
