Apple's September 9 event, the first keynote under new CEO John Ternus, broke the usual pattern. Apple launched two new iPhones: the **iPhone 18 Pro** (from $1,749 CAD) and the **iPhone 18 Pro Max** (from $1,899 CAD). There is no standard iPhone 18. It is reportedly coming in spring 2027, which Apple has not confirmed. (Apple also announced its first foldable, the iPhone Duo, but that gets its own article when it goes on sale, where we'll put it against Samsung's foldable.)

The other headline is price. The Pros cost $150 CAD more than the 17 Pros did at launch, and on the same day Apple quietly *raised* prices on every older iPhone it still sells. The iPhone 17 went from $1,129 to $1,299. There is no longer a cheap iPhone at Apple retail, and the usual "just buy last year's model" advice needs serious rethinking this year.

**Prices checked September 28, 2026:** all prices below are Apple Store Canada outright prices for the 256GB base configuration, confirmed across Apple's Canadian store page, MobileSyrup, and iPhoneInCanada. Carrier pricing differs: Freedom Mobile matches Apple at $1,749 for the Pro, while Rogers, Bell, and Telus list it around $1,799. Note that Canada is reportedly one of the regions where the 18 Pro ships eSIM-only with no physical SIM tray, per Apple's press materials, so frequent travelers who swap physical SIMs should plan around that.

## How this roundup was built

This is a **research roundup, not a hands-on review**. Maple Gadget didn't receive or test these phones in person. Our launch budget is $0, and we won't pretend otherwise. Instead, the rankings below are built from:

- **Measurement-based reviews** from outlets with real testing: The Verge, Tom's Guide (including their standardized battery test), CNET, GSMArena's lab tests (battery endurance, charging, display measurements), DxOMark's camera scoring, PetaPixel's camera analysis, Engadget, PCMag, and Wired
- **Apple's spec pages and newsroom**, used for specifications and Canadian pricing only, never as proof that a product performs well
- **Canadian retail listings** at Apple Store Canada and Best Buy Canada, plus Canadian outlets (MobileSyrup, iPhoneInCanada) for pricing and availability

What we did *not* use: carrier marketing, content-farm "top 10" listicles, AI-generated review spam, or sponsored reviews without clear disclosure. Reddit threads were consulted only as background on common complaints, never as the basis for a ranking.

The criteria, in order: **measured camera performance** (DxOMark scores, reviewer photo comparisons), **measured battery life**, **performance and thermals**, then **Canadian price and availability**. Benchmark chart-topping that doesn't show up in real use was ignored.

Scores are Maple Gadget's own judgment based on the evidence above. Nobody paid for placement, and nobody can. We have no affiliate relationships, and rankings are never for sale.

## The picks

### 1. iPhone 18 Pro Max, best for most people

**Score: 8.8/10. From $1,899 CAD (256GB).**

If you're buying a new iPhone this fall and the size doesn't scare you, this is the default pick. It has the exact same camera system as the smaller Pro, a 6.9-inch display, and the longest battery life Apple has ever claimed: up to 45 hours of video playback. In Tom's Guide's standardized web-browsing test it lasted 18 hours 27 minutes, the best result they've recorded from an iPhone, and Wired got nearly two days of real-world use at around 5 hours of screen time per day.

The camera is the real story this generation. The main 48MP sensor now has a **variable physical aperture** (f/1.48 to f/4.0, with six actual blades), a first for iPhone. DxOMark scored the 18 Pro camera 172 overall, second best of any phone they've tested, and its video score of 176 is the highest they've ever given. PetaPixel's testing found f/2.8 is the sharpness sweet spot, and the variable aperture genuinely fixed the 17 Pro's problem with soft group portraits. Low-light shooting (DxOMark 150) leads their entire ranking.

The honest downsides: it's $150 more than the 17 Pro Max was, it's heavier at 249g, the telephoto barely improved (DxOMark moved it from 141 to 143, still far behind the best Android zoom), and Siri's AI features remain in beta and underwhelming. Reviewer consensus, from The Verge to Engadget, lands on "excellent but not exciting," and every major outlet agrees iPhone 17 Pro owners should skip this generation.

**Pros:** Best iPhone camera ever measured (DxOMark 172); record iPhone battery life; same camera system as the smaller Pro; variable aperture is genuinely useful.

**Cons:** $150 price hike; heavy at 249g; telephoto still trails Android flagships; no reason for 17 Pro owners to upgrade.

**Best for:** Anyone who wants the best iPhone and is fine with a big phone.

**Check price:** [Apple Store Canada](https://www.apple.com/ca/iphone/) · [Best Buy Canada](https://www.bestbuy.ca/en-ca/product/telus-apple-iphone-18-pro-256gb-black-monthly-financing/20069871) (carrier financing)

### 2. iPhone 18 Pro, best for smaller hands

**Score: 8.5/10. From $1,749 CAD (256GB).**

This is the same phone as the Pro Max in everything that matters: same A20 Pro chip, same triple 48MP camera system with variable aperture, same C2 modem, same vapor chamber cooling. The differences are the 6.3-inch display, a smaller battery (Apple claims 36 hours video playback, up from 33), and 211g in the hand instead of 249g.

The battery is the reason this pick exists. Small Pro iPhones used to mean battery anxiety; this year PCMag wrote that the 18 Pro "finally killed my small phone battery anxiety," and Tom's Guide measured 16 hours 17 minutes in their web test, about an hour better than the 17 Pro. GSMArena's lab test gave it a 16:43 active use score, beating the Galaxy S26 and Pixel 11 Pro at similar size.

Buy the Pro Max if you're torn: it's only $150 more and the battery gap is real. Buy this one if you know big phones annoy you.

**Pros:** Full Pro camera system in a manageable size; battery anxiety finally gone on the small Pro; same chip and modem as the Max.

**Cons:** $150 price hike; noticeably less battery than the Max for only $150 less; heavier than its predecessor.

**Best for:** People who want everything the Pro Max does without carrying a 6.9-inch phone.

**Check price:** [Apple Store Canada](https://www.apple.com/ca/iphone/) · [Best Buy Canada](https://www.bestbuy.ca/en-ca/product/telus-apple-iphone-18-pro-256gb-black-monthly-financing/20069871) (carrier financing)

### 3. iPhone 17 on clearance, best value (if you can find it)

**Score: 8.0/10 as a value play. Now $1,299 at Apple (was $1,129); hunt carrier and retailer clearance.**

This is the uncomfortable pick. Apple *raised* the iPhone 17's price by $170 on event day and discontinued the 17 Pro line entirely, so the traditional "last year's model gets cheaper" play is broken at Apple retail. The value now lives wherever carriers and retailers are clearing old stock: carrier clearance pricing, Best Buy Canada open-box and clearance units, and trade-in stacking.

Is it worth hunting? The 17 is still an excellent phone, and it will get years of iOS updates. What you give up versus the 18 Pro: the variable aperture camera, the 2nm A20 Pro (the 17's chip is still fast for everything real), the C2 modem, and some battery life. None of that matters much if the discount is deep enough. What doesn't work: paying Apple's new $1,299 price for it. At that point you're $450 from an 18 Pro, and the math falls apart.

**Pros:** Still a flagship-grade phone; years of software support ahead; the only way to spend meaningfully less than $1,749 on a current iPhone.

**Cons:** Apple raised its price instead of cutting it; no variable aperture; clearance stock is finite and scattered; no 17 Pro option at Apple anymore.

**Best for:** Patient deal hunters willing to check carrier clearance pages and Best Buy Canada rather than Apple.

**Check price:** [Apple Store Canada](https://www.apple.com/ca/iphone/) · carrier clearance pages (Rogers, Bell, Telus, Freedom Mobile)

## Who should wait

**iPhone 17 Pro and 16 Pro owners: skip.** Engadget, CNET, and 9to5Mac are unusually blunt about this. The 18 Pro is a meaningful upgrade starting at the iPhone 15 Pro or older, and especially at the 14 Pro or older, which miss Apple Intelligence features entirely.

**If you normally buy the regular iPhone: you're waiting until spring.** There is no iPhone 18, and multiple outlets report the standard model lands in February or March 2027 alongside an iPhone 18e. Apple hasn't confirmed this, so treat it as well-sourced reporting, not a promise. Your options until then are a discounted 17 or sitting tight.

**The iPhone Air looks like a dead branch.** It sold only 7.7 million units in four quarters per IDC data, under 1.8% of Apple's sales, and no successor was announced. We wouldn't buy into this line now.

**The 20th-anniversary iPhone is rumored for 2027** with a major redesign, which several outlets cite as a reason to wait if your current phone still works. One more year on a battery replacement is the cheapest upgrade Apple sells.

## The bottom line

This is a strange launch: two good phones, no base model, and higher prices across the board. The iPhone 18 Pro Max is the best iPhone for most buyers, the 18 Pro is the right call for smaller hands, and the smartest money this fall might be a discounted iPhone 17 if you can find one. If your current iPhone is a 16 Pro or newer, the kindest thing anyone can tell you is: keep your money.
