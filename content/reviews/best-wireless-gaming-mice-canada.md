## How this roundup was built

This is a **research roundup, not a hands-on review**. Maple Gadget didn't receive or test these mice in person. Our launch budget is $0, and we won't pretend otherwise. Instead, the rankings below are built from:

- **Measurement-based reviews** from outlets with real testing rigs: RTINGS, Tom's Hardware, TechPowerUp, PC Gamer, PCWorld, Tom's Guide, and Windows Central
- **Manufacturer spec sheets**, used for specifications only and never as proof that a product performs well
- **Canadian retail listings** at Best Buy Canada and Amazon.ca for pricing and availability

What we did *not* use: Amazon customer review text, user star ratings, content-farm "top 10" listicles, AI-generated review spam, or sponsored reviews without clear disclosure. Reddit threads were consulted only as background on common complaints, never as the basis for a ranking.

The criteria, in order: **measured performance** (click latency, sensor accuracy), **shape and hand fit**, **weight**, **battery life**, then **Canadian price and availability**. Headline DPI was ignored as a criterion, because 12,000 DPI is already far more than anyone needs.

Scores are Maple Gadget's own judgment based on the evidence above. Nobody paid for placement, and nobody can. We have no affiliate relationships, and rankings are never for sale.

## The picks

### 1. Logitech G305 Lightspeed, best overall

**Score: 8.6/10. Around $50–60 CAD.**

The G305 is the safest recommendation in budget wireless, and it's not close. RTINGS' 2026 budget guide calls it the best budget wireless gaming mouse for most people, citing excellent click latency, reliable sensor performance, sturdy construction, and a shape that suits most hands and grips. Tom's Hardware praised its advanced HERO 12K sensor and rock-solid Lightspeed wireless connection. The honest downsides: at around 100g with its AA battery installed it's heavy by 2026 lightweight standards, it runs on a disposable AA (250 hours, so you'll replace it a few times a year), and there's no Bluetooth, just the 2.4GHz dongle.

None of that matters much at CA$50–60. It just works, everywhere, for years. This is the mouse to buy when you don't want to think about it.

**Pros:** Excellent measured click latency; reliable sensor; sturdy build; shape suits most hands; outstanding battery life.

**Cons:** Heavy by modern standards at ~100g; disposable AA battery; no Bluetooth; small for large hands.

**Best for:** Anyone who wants a proven wireless gaming mouse that just works.

**Check price:** [Amazon.ca](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B07CMS5Q6P) · [Best Buy Canada](https://www.bestbuy.ca/en-ca/product/logitech-g305-12000-dpi-wireless-optical-gaming-mouse-black/12642295)

### 2. Razer Orochi V2, best ultralight for small hands

**Score: 8.2/10. Around $40–55 CAD.**

The Orochi V2 is what the G305 would be if it went on a diet: around 70g with a battery installed versus the G305's 100g, plus Bluetooth alongside Razer's HyperSpeed 2.4GHz wireless, and up to 425 hours of battery over the dongle. TechPowerUp said it one-upped the G305 through much lower weight without sacrificing build quality, and Laptop Mag praised its performance, dual wireless modes, and battery life. The catch is the size: it's a genuinely small mouse. TechPowerUp's reviewer found it caused cramping, and it's really a fingertip or claw grip mouse for small to medium hands. Large hands should skip it.

At around CA$40–55 in Canada, it's the cheapest way to get a truly lightweight wireless mouse from a major brand.

**Pros:** Very light at ~70g; dual wireless (2.4GHz + Bluetooth); huge battery life; solid build quality.

**Cons:** Tiny, too small for large hands; fingertip/claw grip really required; can cause cramping on bigger hands.

**Best for:** Small hands and fingertip or claw grippers who want ultralight on a budget.

**Check price:** [Amazon.ca](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B0916N2LPZ) · [Best Buy Canada](https://www.bestbuy.ca/en-ca/product/razer-orochi-v2-18000-dpi-wireless-optical-gaming-mouse-black/15446909)

## What to look for (and what to ignore)

**Shape and hand fit matter more than specs at this price.** Every mouse here tracks well enough for casual and mid-level competitive play. Pick by grip style (palm, claw, fingertip) and hand size first. An 8.6 mouse in the wrong shape for your hand is worse than a 7.5 in the right one.

**Decide your battery tradeoff before you buy.** Disposable AA or AAA batteries last months and let you keep gaming with a quick swap, but they add weight. Built-in rechargeable designs run lighter but need regular charging. There's no wrong answer, just the one that annoys you less.

**Don't overpay for headline DPI.** 12,000 DPI is already far more than anyone needs. Sensor accuracy, click latency, and a stable 1,000Hz polling rate matter more in real games, and all six picks do 1,000Hz over their 2.4GHz dongles.

**For competitive shooters, lighter is genuinely better.** The G305's ~100g is heavy by 2026 standards, which is exactly why the Orochi V2 (~70g) and Haste 2 Core (70g) exist at this price. Less mass to move means faster, less tiring aim over long sessions.

**Check stock and price the week you buy.** These mice swing between CA$40 and CA$100 on Amazon.ca and Best Buy Canada, and the best pick changes with the sale. The DeathAdder V2 X was CA$39.99 at Best Buy Canada until mid-September and is CA$79.46 on Amazon.ca now; the Haste 2 Core was CA$48.98 until September 17. Patience pays, and our deals page tracks the ones actually on sale.

If you buy one mouse from this list, make it the **Logitech G305 Lightspeed** (around $50–60). It's the safest pick in budget wireless: proven sensor, excellent click latency, great battery life, and a shape most hands get along with. Boring, reliable, cheap.

If the G305 feels heavy or small, the **Razer Orochi V2** (around $40–55) is the lightweight answer for small hands. Feature hunters should wait for the **Basilisk V3 X HyperSpeed** to drop back under $75 before considering it, and absolute bargain hunters should watch for the **HyperX Pulsefire Haste 2 Core Wireless** on sale under $50.
